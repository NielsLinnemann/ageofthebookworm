Heuristics and Strategies in Philosophy of Physics

100 recurring strategies extracted from 50 BJPS philosophy-of-physics papers (2016–2026) by AI agents — Claude, GPT-4o, and Gemini. No human read and classified the papers; everything here is model output.

The survey

The survey reads full texts and abstracts of 50 BJPS philosophy-of-physics papers from 2016 to 2026 and extracts the reusable moves papers are built from. Each strategy is scored from 1 to 5 on three axes:

  • Creativity (C): how much new philosophical content the move produces. 5 means seeing something nobody saw; 1 means filling in a template.
  • Usefulness (U): how often opportunities arise and how reliably they turn into publishable work.
  • Mechanisability (M): how well a language model with a corpus and search could run the move to generate candidate ideas (not finished papers).

The scores are editorial judgements, not measurements, and so is the grouping by operation and altitude.

Main findings

The axes pull in the dull direction. Creativity correlates negatively with usefulness (−0.43) and with mechanisability (−0.40); usefulness and mechanisability go together (+0.44). On average, what a model can run is neither very creative nor hard to execute. A generator optimised for "would this be publishable" converges there and produces correct, well-placed, inert papers.

The exceptions are the interesting part. Sixteen strategies score 5 on creativity, and most of them are hard to mechanise. Three are not: rejected-branch retrieval, motivating-case failure and complement construction. In each, the step that looks creative is really a search nobody has run: over options authors explicitly set aside, over the scientific cases a programme cites as its own vindication, over the preconditions of canonical objections.

The blind spot is about the operation, not the level. Debates' option spaces (scorecards, taxonomies) are among the easiest targets for a model; the hard level is the field itself (agenda, practice, institutions). Operation predicts better: occupying and splitting an already-mapped option space is the most mechanisable, while constituting something new is the least.

More than half are highly mechanisable. 54 of the 100 strategies score 4 or 5 on mechanisability — a language model with a corpus and search could plausibly run the move to generate candidate ideas. 16 score the maximum (M=5): these are almost entirely search or gap-finding moves. The remaining 50 require genuine philosophical judgement — recognising what is load-bearing, what counts as a good explanation, what the field actually needs.

One strategy is demonstrably new. Pointing philosophy's apparatus at large language models has essentially no instances before 2024 and dozens afterwards.

Five most mechanisable strategies

The strategies below score M=5 and are ranked by creativity. Each represents a move a language model could plausibly run as a search over a corpus — the creative work is finding the right gap or structural match, not generating genuinely new philosophical content.

Rejected-branch retrieval
Mechanisability 5/5 · Creativity 5/5 · Usefulness 3/5

Defend the interpretation the original author explicitly considered and discarded.

What it delivers

When an author proposed a view, they also considered an alternative and set it aside. You argue that the alternative was rejected too quickly and is in fact worth defending. This only counts as a contribution if you make the positive case for it; simply pointing out that the option existed is a historical curiosity.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Simon Friederich, Introducing the Q-Based Interpretation of Quantum Theory (2021)
  • Christian Wüthrich, Craig Callender, What Becomes of a Causal Set? (2016)
Cross-field programme import
Mechanisability 5/5 · Creativity 4/5 · Usefulness 4/5

Bring a live research programme from another subfield and show it dissolves an impasse.

What it delivers

A debate has stalled. You bring in a well-developed research approach from another area of philosophy or science and show that it gets the debate moving again. The connection between the two areas is then available for others to use. The imported approach must not bring commitments the original debate will refuse to accept.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Alexander Franklin, Vanessa A. Seifert, The Problem of Molecular Structure Just Is the Measurement Problem (2021)
  • Emilia Margoni, Daniele Oriti, The Emergence of Spacetime: What Role for Functionalism? (2025)
Argument-isomorphism audit
Mechanisability 5/5 · Creativity 4/5 · Usefulness 3/5

Ask whether two arguments in different debates are structurally the same argument.

What it delivers

You show that two arguments from separate debates have the same underlying structure. Objections and replies developed in one debate then apply directly in the other, which can save both sides a great deal of work. The match has to be real; if it only holds once the details are stripped away, the conclusion is much weaker.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Alexander Franklin, Vanessa A. Seifert, The Problem of Molecular Structure Just Is the Measurement Problem (2021)
  • Oliver Pooley, James Alexander Mabyn Read, On the Mathematics and Metaphysics of the Hole Argument (2021)
Hub-and-spoke piping
Mechanisability 5/5 · Creativity 3/5 · Usefulness 5/5

Take one physical result and pipe it into every metaphysical debate downstream of it — one paper per spoke.

What it delivers

You take one important result from physics and work out its consequences for each of several philosophical debates, typically one paper per debate. The first application takes the most effort; later ones go faster. Each paper must engage seriously with its own debate, not just restate the physics.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Sheldon Goldstein, Ward Struyve et al., The Bohmian Approach to the Problems of Cosmological Quantum Fluctuations (2022)
  • Charles T. Sebens, The Mass of the Gravitational Field (2019)
Definite-article pluralisation
Mechanisability 5/5 · Creativity 3/5 · Usefulness 4/5

A field refers to something in the singular — the law, the problem, the argument. Show there are several.

What it delivers

A field talks about 'the' problem, 'the' law or 'the' argument as if there were only one. You show that the term actually covers several distinct things. Writers then have to say which one they mean, which removes a source of confusion. The distinct versions should lead to different conclusions; otherwise the distinction changes little.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Katie Robertson, In Search of the Holy Grail: How to Reduce the Second Law of Thermodynamics (2021)

Five least mechanisable strategies

After removing strategies that depend on personal circumstances rather than on finding a philosophical idea, the least mechanisable moves are all M=2 with C=5 — meaning they are among the most creative in the survey but resist automation. The key move in each case requires a kind of lateral judgement that corpus search cannot replicate: seeing that a presupposition was optional, that a norm defeats itself, that two debates are structurally the same thing.

Reconciliation by presupposition rejection
Mechanisability 2/5 · Creativity 5/5 · Usefulness 2/5

Reject a presupposition all parties share; the rival positions turn out to be compatible.

What it delivers

You show that two rival positions both depend on an assumption that neither actually needs. Once the assumption is dropped, the two positions turn out to be compatible, so the dispute dissolves instead of being won. This is rare and often welcome, but whatever you put in place of the shared assumption must be less controversial than the assumption itself.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Christian Wüthrich, Craig Callender, What Becomes of a Causal Set? (2016)
  • Oliver Pooley, James Alexander Mabyn Read, On the Mathematics and Metaphysics of the Hole Argument (2021)
Self-undermining charge
Mechanisability 2/5 · Creativity 5/5 · Usefulness 2/5

Find the case where adopting a norm violates the very value the norm was meant to express.

What it delivers

You show that following a particular rule or standard would, in some case, damage the very thing the rule was meant to protect. That is a strong objection, because it uses the rule's own purpose against it. Good cases are hard to find: most rules do not undermine their own aims in any clear way.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Gerardo S. Ciepielewski, Elias Okon et al., On Superdeterministic Rejections of Settings Independence (2021)
  • Richard Dawid, Simon Friederich, Epistemic Separability and Everettian Branches: A Critique of Sebens and Carroll (2020)
Convergence / practical irrelevance
Mechanisability 2/5 · Creativity 5/5 · Usefulness 3/5

Show that a distinction people fight over stops mattering under realistic conditions.

What it delivers

People argue fiercely over a distinction. You show that, under realistic conditions, both sides give the same answers in practice. The theoretical disagreement may remain, but it stops mattering for any practical decision. This is especially valuable when the disagreement has been used to justify different practical choices.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Katie Robertson, Alastair Wilson, Theoretical Relicts: Progress, Reduction, and Autonomy (2023)
Representation-vs-reality deflation
Mechanisability 2/5 · Creativity 5/5 · Usefulness 4/5

Replace the numbers in a formalism with the properties they are supposed to represent, and see which results survive.

What it delivers

Mathematical descriptions of physical systems use numbers and structures that may not correspond directly to anything real. You rewrite the description in terms of the physical properties themselves and check which conclusions survive. Often some apparently deep conclusions turn out to come from the notation rather than from the world.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Oliver Pooley, James Alexander Mabyn Read, On the Mathematics and Metaphysics of the Hole Argument (2021)
  • Lu Chen, Symmetries as Isomorphisms (2024)
Doctrine-to-stance reclassification
Mechanisability 2/5 · Creativity 5/5 · Usefulness 3/5

Argue that a position is the wrong logical type — not a thesis about the world but a methodological stance — using its adherents' behaviour as the evidence.

What it delivers

You argue that a position presented as a claim about the world is really better understood as an attitude or method — a way of going about inquiry. This changes what kinds of arguments are relevant to it. It is worth doing only if the reclassification actually changes how the debate should proceed.

Example papers (BJPS)
  • Simon Friederich, Introducing the Q-Based Interpretation of Quantum Theory (2021)
  • Roman Frigg, Charlotte Werndl, Can Somebody Please Say What Gibbsian Statistical Mechanics Says? (2018)

All strategies

This study was produced entirely by AI agents. The strategy extraction, scoring, and example assignment were carried out by Claude Sonnet and Claude Opus (Anthropic), with GPT-4o (OpenAI) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (Google) used for cross-checking the scoring dimensions. No human read through the papers and classified what they were doing; the strategies, scores, and assignments are entirely model output. They may be wrong in ways that human coding would have caught. Example papers in particular are machine-made assignments: inclusion does not mean the authors would describe their work this way, and it says nothing about the quality of the work. Use the results as a starting point, not as a verdict.

How the papers were selected

All OpenAlex "article" records from BJPS, 2016–2026 (565 papers) were screened in two stages: first by Claude Haiku on title and abstract (philosophy of physics? research article?), then by Claude Sonnet on the full abstract, retaining only clearly conceptual or philosophical papers (128 eligible). A seeded random draw balanced over three periods (2016–19, 2020–23, 2024–26), with at most two papers per first author per period. Only papers with a findable open copy (publisher OA, arXiv, institutional repository) were kept; 73 drawn papers had no open copy and were dropped. The 50 papers are effectively all BJPS philosophy-of-physics papers from 2016–2026 with a freely accessible full text.
